## Background

Continuum robots are robots that have continuous structures, allowing them to bend and flex similarly to biological appendages such as elephant trunks or octopus arms. This flexibility allows continuum robots to navigate complex environments and perform tasks that traditional rigid-link robots cannot. However, the very properties that make continuum robots advantageous also present significant challenges in accurately estimating their state (e.g., shape and velocity) during operation.

## Project Description

My research focuses on developing continuous-time state estimation techniques for continuum robots. Unlike traditional discrete-time methods, continuous-time approaches can provide more accurate and smooth estimates of the robot's state by leveraging the continuous nature of their structure. This involves formulating mathematical models that capture the dynamics of continuum robots and designing algorithms that can process sensor data in real-time to estimate their state accurately.
